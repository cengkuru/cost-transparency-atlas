From published records to public accountability

A procurement ID is the start of a trail.

Can someone follow it far enough to ask a useful question?

A project description tells us what is planned. A procurement connection can help us inspect who was chosen and on what terms. Those are different steps.

A closer look at 107 records from NCC Zambia. This is one publisher's saved package, not a measure of national coverage or a country ranking.

01 · Find a connection

13 records give us a procurement identifier.

In the other 94 of 107 records, this field cannot provide a route from the project to a contracting process.

An identifier is a place to begin looking. Its presence alone does not tell us whether a source opens, whether the relationship is correct, or whether delivery happened.

02 · Ask two questions together

Only 11 records contain both a budget and a procurement ID.

The package contains 81 budgets with currency and 13 records with procurement identifiers. Looking at those totals separately cannot tell us where they meet.

Here they meet in 11 of 107 records. A reader can start investigating the planned amount alongside the procurement connection. This is not a measure of money spent.

Calculated · Same-record intersection · 11 / 107 · 5 Sep 2026

03 · Make the missing connections visible

A budget can be visible while procurement stays unlinked.

70 records have a budget with currency but no procurement ID. Two have an ID without that budget pair. Another 24 have neither.

These are different follow-up questions for a publisher. They cannot be resolved by one headline completeness percentage.

05 · Turn the gap into a testable next step

Support the connection that lets someone check.

Before funding a wider cleanup, a publisher and an assurance lead could check a small sample together: does each connection open, identify the same project, and answer the question being asked?
